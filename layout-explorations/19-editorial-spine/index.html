Design systems need a reading layer.

A calm default for ideas that need room to develop: strong title, ordinary prose, a few useful interruptions, and no decorative obligation.

By Design Lasagna8 min readUpdated 2026-09-06

Design-system work often arrives as a specification, a component, or a pile of decisions. Writing gives those decisions a legible order. The layout should support that job before it tries to perform a mood.

That means maintaining a dependable reading measure even when the subject is technical. A page can still make room for a diagram, a comparison, or a quote; those moments should clarify the argument rather than compete with it.

Make the claim first.

A reader should understand the argument from the standfirst and first section. A narrow prose measure keeps the work comfortable; a wider canvas remains available for information that cannot fit in a paragraph.

“The visual system should make a complex idea easier to enter, not make the idea wait for the visual system.”

Claims also need an order. Start with the decision, explain the constraint that made it necessary, and then show the evidence. This sequence lets a reader skim confidently and return to the detail when the decision affects their own work.

Let evidence break the column.

A figure, a short comparison, or an annotated example can reset the pace. It should be part of the argument, with a caption that explains why it is here.

Breakout material earns its width by carrying more information than a paragraph can. When it does not, the reading column is usually the more useful place for it.

Keep the interface quiet.

Most of the page should not announce itself. Consistent headings, links, and metadata allow the reader to recognize the structure without relearning it in every section.

ElementPurposeBest use
ProseDevelop an ideaExplanation and rationale
FigureMake a relationship visibleProcess and comparison
CalloutPreserve a constraintWarnings and decisions

The table is wider because comparison is its job. The surrounding interpretation remains in the same dependable spine, so the page returns to a familiar pace immediately afterward.

End with a route onward.

The conclusion does not need to repeat every point. It should close the thought and offer the next relevant guide, reference, or related article.

A reading layer is therefore not a neutral absence of design. It is a deliberate promise: readers can find the argument, understand its evidence, and leave with a useful next step.
