Writing · diptych

A diptych layout for articles with strong imagery.

Text and image share equal weight, making it good for interviews, walkthroughs, and stories where the visuals should feel like chapters.

Open with an image, then answer the question.

The visual side of the pair can pose the question, while the text side explains the detail. That makes the article feel structured without becoming static.

Swap sides to keep the page moving.

Alternating the image position creates a gentle rhythm. It also makes the article feel more like a designed narrative than a list of sections.

Use captions to bridge the gap.

Each image gets a short label or caption, so the reader always knows why it is there. That helps the page remain editorial instead of ornamental.

Notes

Best for: interviews, profiles, and story pages with strong visuals.

Pattern: lead image → paired chapters → closing note.
