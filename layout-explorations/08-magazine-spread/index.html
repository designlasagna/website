Writing · magazine spread

Make the page feel like a feature.

A layered article layout for site introductions, featured essays, and guide pages that benefit from a little more visual drama.

At a glance

Large title, short intro, and a supporting panel. This gives the page a magazine-like opening while still keeping the content grounded in article structure.

Compared with a strict column layout, this one opens the page up. The hero takes up space, the body can mix blocks and commentary, and the sidebar can behave like a note from the editor.

Lead visualUse a large image or graphic area here when the page needs a stronger first impression.
Summary blockA quick overview card that distills the article into three sentences.
Side noteExtra context, related links, or a small call to action.

Open with energy, then settle down.

The trick is to front-load visual interest without making the rest of the page exhausting. After the hero, the article should become more legible and familiar: readable paragraphs, section headers, and a few well-placed supporting elements.

This makes the layout useful for launch posts, essays with examples, and “what we’re building” pages where tone matters as much as structure.

A good feature layout says: stay with me, there’s substance here.

Editorial, but not ornamental.

Everything still serves the writing. The color palette is bold but restrained, the whitespace is generous, and the side notes are there to help the reader orient themselves quickly.

Notes from the editor

Best for: feature articles, release stories, and pages with a bit of narrative flair.

Helpful devices: pull quotes, summary panels, and image-like cards.
